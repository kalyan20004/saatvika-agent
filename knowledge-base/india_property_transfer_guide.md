# India Property Transfer After Death Guide
> SYNTHETIC DOCUMENT — For demonstration purposes only. Not real legal advice.

## Overview
Transferring immovable property (land, house, flat) after death requires mutation (Dakhil Kharij) in revenue records. This is separate from legal ownership — mutation changes the name in government records.

## Two Scenarios

### Scenario A: Deceased Left a Will (Testate Succession)
1. Probate required if Will is challenged OR for properties in Mumbai, Chennai, West Bengal (mandatory)
2. In other states, Will + Death Certificate usually sufficient for mutation
3. Executor named in Will initiates transfer

### Scenario B: No Will (Intestate Succession)
1. Succession governed by personal law:
   - Hindus: Hindu Succession Act, 1956
   - Muslims: Muslim Personal Law (Shariat) Application Act, 1937
   - Christians/Parsis: Indian Succession Act, 1925
2. Class I heirs (spouse, children, mother) inherit first
3. Legal Heir Certificate / Succession Certificate required

## Mutation Process (Dakhil Kharij)

### Documents Required for Mutation
- Death Certificate of deceased owner
- Legal Heir Certificate (from Tehsildar/Revenue Officer)
- Succession Certificate (if contested or for movable property)
- Affidavit by all legal heirs
- NOC from other legal heirs (if single heir claiming)
- Original property documents (sale deed, title deed)
- Property tax receipts (up to date)
- Encumbrance Certificate

### Step-by-Step Mutation Process
1. Apply at local Revenue Office / Tehsildar's office
2. Submit application with all documents
3. Revenue Officer issues public notice (30 days for objections)
4. If no objection: mutation order issued
5. Name updated in Record of Rights (RoR/Pahani)
6. Get certified copies of updated records

## Society Flat (Apartment) Transfer
- Inform Housing Society Secretary in writing
- Submit Death Certificate + Share Certificate
- Society passes resolution for share transfer
- New share certificate issued in heir's name
- No stamp duty for inheritance transfer

## Home Loan Outstanding
- If home loan is outstanding: notify bank immediately
- Check if home loan had insurance (HLPP — Home Loan Protection Plan)
- If insured: claim settles outstanding loan
- If not insured: heir inherits property AND loan liability

## Capital Gains Tax on Inherited Property
- No tax on inheritance itself
- If heir sells the property: Capital Gains Tax applies
- Cost of acquisition = original purchase price by deceased
- Indexation benefit available
